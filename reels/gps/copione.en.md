# How GPS knows where you are

> Same [scenes] and [marks] as copione.md: the scenes re-sync to this language by themselves.

[scena:aggancio]
*GPS* satellites [nonsa]have no idea where you are. Your phone sends them [niente]nothing: it just [ascolta]*listens*.

[scena:orbita]
[sopra]Above you, [km]*twenty* thousand kilometers up, fly more than [sat]*thirty* satellites. Each one repeats [due]*two* things: [dove]where it is and [ora]what time it is.

[scena:distanza]
The [parte]signal travels at the speed of *light*: it arrives in about [ms]*seventy* milliseconds. From that [ritardo]delay, your phone works out the [dist]*distance*.

[scena:cerchi]
[uno]One satellite puts you on a *circle*. [tre]Three circles cross at a single [punto]*point*. The [quarto]*fourth* corrects [orologio]your phone's clock.

[scena:tempo]
One *absurd* detail: [veloce]up there, time runs *faster*, by [micro]*thirty-eight* millionths of a second a day. It sounds like [poco]nothing, but at the [luce]speed of light that's about [dieci]*ten* kilometers of error per day. [percio]That's why satellite clocks are [einstein]corrected with *Einstein's* equations.
